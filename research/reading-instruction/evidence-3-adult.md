# Section 3 — Adult and Postsecondary Readers

Scope: adult basic education, postsecondary developmental reading, and adult learners reading
below grade level. Focus on what changes for adults and what the app must do differently.

## What changes for adults versus adolescents

| Dimension | Adolescent | Adult | App consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Motivation | Interest and peer engagement | Relevance, autonomy, self-efficacy, prior experience | Choose adult-relevant topics; explain the purpose of the skill once. |
| Time | Scheduled class time | Fragmented, self-directed | Keep sessions short and complete; one skill per lesson. |
| Word-level needs | Variable | More varied; some need decoding and morphology | Inline support, vocabulary items, no assumptions. |
| Tone | Respectful, not childish | Respectful, not remedial | Same rule, higher stakes. |
| Assessment | Grades and progress monitoring | Often high-stakes or self-referred | No grade, no rank, no implied placement. |

## Design consequences for this app

1. Adult lessons must be self-contained and purposeful. The reader should know what move they are
   practicing and why it matters. [19, 33]
2. Do not use a passage because it is "simple." Use it because the idea is worth reading and the
   task fits the band. [32, 36]
3. Vocabulary support is not a separate remedial track. It is part of comprehension. [28, 31]
4. Feedback names the evidence and the move. It never praises the person. [34]
5. The ticket is a record of practice, not a diagnosis. [29, 33]

## Open issues from this section

- Most adult evidence is about programs, not one-shot AI lessons. Transfer is an assumption.
- No source yet on optimal passage length for adult self-directed sessions.
- The app cannot offer learner choice of text; the onboarding category choice is the only
  autonomy point. Note this as a design limitation.
